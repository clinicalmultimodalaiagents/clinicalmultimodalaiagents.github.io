MCA 2027: 1st Workshop on Multimodal AI Agents for Biomedical Imaging and Clinical Decision Support

Introduction

Clinical decisions rarely rest on a single image. Clinicians combine imaging with vital signs, laboratory results, medication histories, and free-text notes that change over hours and days. Multimodal foundation models represent the heterogeneous modalities of image, text and structured records jointly, while agentic frameworks offer the control layer that decides what to look at next. Together, they can now plan, call tools, retrieve evidence, and reason across these diverse modalities. This workshop brings together researchers in medical imaging, clinical machine learning, and agentic AI to examine how multimodal agents should be designed, grounded, evaluated, and deployed for clinical decision support.

Topics of Interest

Foundation models and vision-language models have advanced medical image analysis quickly. Clinical decisions, however, are multimodal and longitudinal: an ICU clinician reads a chest radiograph alongside ventilator settings, lab trends, and nursing notes accumulated across a stay. Agentic systems that plan, call specialized tools such as segmentation or retrieval models, and combine the evidence are a natural fit for this setting. How should agents ground their claims in image regions? How should they handle patient histories that exceed context limits? How do we evaluate the reasoning and not only the final answer? How do these systems fail across hospitals and scanners? We welcome works that address these questions across imaging, clinical machine learning, and agentic AI, including but not limited to:

  • Multimodal fusion of imaging with EHR, physiological time series, and clinical text
  • Agent architectures, tool use, and multi-agent systems for diagnosis, triage, and prognosis
  • Long-context and memory mechanisms for patient trajectories
  • Spatial, anatomical, and visual grounding of agent outputs
  • Benchmarks and evaluation protocols that reflect clinical workflows
  • Robustness, uncertainty, and generalization across sites and scanners
  • Human-AI interaction, safety, and clinical translation
